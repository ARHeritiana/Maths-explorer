The Hiker Problem

Problem. On Day 1, a hiker leaves the foot of a mountain at 08:00 and reaches the summit at 20:00. On Day 2, he leaves the summit at 08:00 and descends by the same trail, arriving at the foot at 20:00. His speed is arbitrary on both days. Show that there is at least one point of the trail that he passes at exactly the same time of day on both days.

Full solution

TL;DR. Superimposing the two days gives two walkers moving in opposite directions along one path, so they must meet. That meeting is a place passed at the same time of day on both days.

08:00
Blue: Day 1 ascent. Orange: Day 2 descent. Red: meeting point(s), shown only when revealed and after the hikers have met.
  1. Let u(t) and d(t) be the positions (0 = foot, 1 = summit) at time t on Day 1 and Day 2. Both are continuous on [8, 20].
  2. Define g(t) = u(t) − d(t), continuous on [8, 20].
  3. At 08:00: g = 0 − 1 = −1 < 0. At 20:00: g = 1 − 0 = +1 > 0.
  4. By the Intermediate Value Theorem, some t* has g(t*) = 0, i.e. u(t*) = d(t*): same place, same time of day.
Live: . Generically the number of meetings is odd; monotone walks give exactly one.
