\ExplorerTitle{High school · optimisation and olympiads}{Inequalities and equality cases}
\ExplorerSection{Exercise 1}
For \(x>0\), prove \(x+\frac{1}{x}\geq 2\) and determine equality.\par
\(x+\frac{1}{x}-2=\frac{(x-1)^{2}}{x}\geq 0\). Equality holds exactly at \(x=1\).\par
\ExplorerSection{Exercise 2}
Find the minimum of \(x^{2}-6x+13\) on \(\mathbb{R}\).\par
\(x^{2}-6x+13=(x-3)^{2}+4\geq 4\). The minimum 4 is attained at \(x=3\).\par
\ExplorerSection{Exercise 3}
A rectangle has perimeter 24 m. What is its greatest area?\par
Its sides \(a,b>0\) satisfy \(a+b=12\). Thus \(ab\leq 36\), with equality at \(a=b=6\,\mathrm{m}\). The maximum area is \(36\,\mathrm{m}^{2}\).\par
\ExplorerSection{Exercise 4}
For real a,b, prove \(a^{2}+b^{2}\geq 2ab\). Must a and b be positive?\par
\(a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab=(a-b)^{2}\geq 0\) for all real a,b. No positivity is needed; equality means \(a=b\).\par
\ExplorerSection{Problem: complete reasoning}
There are 40 m of fencing for three sides of a rectangular enclosure against a wall. Its width x, perpendicular to the wall, is positive. Express its length and area in terms of x, determine the domain, then prove the maximum area without derivatives. Compare with a fully fenced rectangle of perimeter 40 m.\par
Length is \(40-2x\) and \(0<x<20\). Area is \(A(x)=x(40-2x)=200-2(x-10)^{2}\leq 200\). Equality occurs at width 10 m and length 20 m: \(200\,\mathrm{m}^{2}\). With all four sides fenced, \(a+b=20\) and the maximum area is \(100\,\mathrm{m}^{2}\), attained by a square of side 10 m.\par
