# Maths-explorer — Improvement Goals and Success Criteria

## Guiding Principles

1. **Mathematics is best learned visually and experimentally.** Learners should see, draw, manipulate, explore, conjecture, and understand mathematical concepts before formalizing them.
2. **Mathematics is rich and interesting.** The website should inspire curiosity through discoveries, surprising connections, history, and engaging problems.

**Priority:** High-school mathematics, with the first principle guiding all improvements.

## Goals and Measurable Success Criteria

### 1. Establish a Strong Visual Identity
Create a coherent, elegant, colorful, and recognizable mathematical design across the entire website.

**Success:** All major sections, resource cards, interactive diagrams, and slides follow a consistent visual language, with no significant inconsistencies in desktop and mobile reviews.

### 2. Make Mathematical Content Visual and Interactive
Every lesson, exercise, problem, challenge, story, and curiosity that would benefit from a diagram, animation, or experiment should have one. Drawing, constructing, and manipulating mathematical objects should feel natural to learners.

**Success:**
- All existing and new content is assessed for opportunities to improve understanding through visualization or experimentation.
- Every item identified as benefiting from interactive visualization has an appropriate, functional implementation.
- Learners can naturally draw, construct, modify, and explore mathematical objects wherever these interactions are relevant.
- Visualizations genuinely support mathematical understanding rather than serving as decoration.

### 3. Expand Interactive Slides
Make interactive presentations an integral part of every lesson, featuring progressive explanations, meaningful animations, and opportunities for experimentation.

**Success:** Every lesson has a complete, mathematically accurate, functional interactive presentation, with appropriate visual explanations and interactive elements wherever relevant.

### 4. Introduce Interactive Error Correction
Create a dedicated collection where learners identify common mathematical mistakes, investigate them, discover counterexamples, and repair incorrect reasoning.

**Success:**
- The collection covers the most common errors and conceptual misunderstandings across the priority high-school curricula.
- Coverage is assessed by mathematical topic and misconception, rather than by the number of activities.
- Every activity provides a clear diagnosis, a mathematically sound correction, and meaningful opportunities for investigation.
- Appropriate errors can be explored through interactive diagrams, counterexamples, or animated reasoning.

### 5. Integrate Error Investigations Throughout the Site
Connect common mistakes and misconceptions with relevant lessons, exercises, problems, and interactive presentations.

**Success:** Every error investigation is independently accessible and appropriately connected to the mathematical concepts and learning resources it concerns, without unnecessary duplication.

### 6. Develop a Grand Oral Section
Present mathematically rich questions connected to interactive explorations, applications, history, rigorous explanations, and presentation preparation.

**Success:** The collection offers broad, meaningful coverage of suitable high-school mathematical topics. Every published topic includes a mathematical investigation, reliable sources, an explanation, presentation guidance, and possible jury questions, with interactive explorations wherever beneficial.

### 7. Strengthen Learning Pathways
Create coherent connections between prerequisites, lessons, experiments, exercises, challenges, and mathematical discoveries.

**Success:** Every priority high-school curriculum offers coherent learning pathways, with clearly identified prerequisites, meaningful progression, and working links between related resources.

### 8. Improve Animations and Direct Manipulation
Make mathematical interactions smooth, intuitive, informative, and visually appealing, while minimizing interface clutter.

**Success:** All interactive activities have accurate mathematical behavior, clear visual feedback, appropriate mouse and touch interactions, and no significant animation or manipulation defects. Drawing and direct manipulation take precedence over unnecessary controls.

### 9. Unify Mathematical Experiences
Ensure related concepts, figures, experiments, and explanations remain consistent across lessons, laboratories, challenges, and slides.

**Success:** Shared mathematical concepts use consistent notation, values, and representations, with no contradictory results or unnecessary duplication across their different presentations.

### 10. Preserve Mathematical Rigor and Accessibility
Ensure visualizations are mathematically correct, distinguish experimental evidence from proof, and remain accessible across devices.

**Success:** Zero known mathematical errors or critical accessibility defects in accepted content; all computed results independently verified; no horizontal overflow at 320 px width or 200% text zoom; all essential interactions keyboard-accessible.

## Overall Acceptance Criteria

- Every mathematical resource is evaluated for meaningful opportunities to visualize, draw, manipulate, or experiment.
- **All resources that would benefit from interactive mathematical representations receive them**, without imposing unnecessary interactions on unsuitable content.
- Every lesson has an interactive presentation.
- Error-correction coverage addresses the principal misconceptions across the high-school curriculum, without arbitrary numerical quotas.
- All new mathematical content meets the project's verification and publication standards.
- Existing features and content remain functional after improvements.
- The high-school learning experience remains the principal development priority.
- Improvements demonstrably strengthen **visual understanding, experimentation, mathematical rigor, and curiosity**, rather than merely increasing the quantity of content.
