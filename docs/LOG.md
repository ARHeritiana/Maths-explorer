- 2026-10-18: new challenge "Trois pions qui sautent" (`pegs`, invariants, supplied by the owner as a React sketch; the problem is added, not the sketch). Three pegs at (0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1); a peg jumps over another to the symmetric point 2B − A if free; the learner reaches for a target point. Checkable version: fewest jumps to reach the chosen point, 0 if impossible (preset targets; default (1, 1), answer 0). Invariant: 2B − A ≡ A (mod 2), so each peg keeps its parity class and (1, 1) is never reachable. Pure maths in `challenge-puzzles2.js` (`pegsSolve` by breadth-first search; the parity shortcut returns null for forbidden targets), activity in `challenge-classics2.js` (tap two pegs, undo, restart, camera that only grows during a game, hint buttons for parity labels and colouring the points by parity), animated solution in `challenge-solutions-3.js`. Tests in `tests/challenge-puzzles2.test.mjs`: exhaustive search to 7 jumps plus 200 random walks of 60 jumps (no parity change, (1, 1) never reached) and an independent iterative-deepening search for the minima (1, 2, 2, 3, 3 for the reachable presets). Proof added the same day at the owner's request: the minima and the reachability of every point of the three parity classes are argued in the worked solution (FR and EN). Four-jump macros slide the whole triple by (2, 0) and (0, 2) (`PEGS_SHIFTS`); a jump is its own inverse and a landing is never blocked, so peg X reaches every X0 + 2(m, n). The lower bounds list the points occupied after at most 1 and 2 jumps (`pegsWithin`, checked by an independent enumeration, and the macros are simulated for |m|, |n| up to 5). The animated solution gained a step for the macro.
