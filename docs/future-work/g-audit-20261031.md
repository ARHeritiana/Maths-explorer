## 1. equidecomposability-of-polygons.html

Mathematics, verified: for 14 presets x 3 partners (42 pairs), at stage times 0, 0.5 and 1, the sum of absolute piece
areas equals 500 (worst error 8e-13). Consecutive stages join up (point-sampling of the union of pieces at the end of
stage i against the start of stage i+1, plus P at start and R at end of both chains: 0 mismatches over 3000 points each).
The 180-degree rotations about midpoints, the shear lemma and the stack into W x A/W behave as the Method text says.
Clockwise or counter-clockwise input, collinear vertices, a vertex lying on an edge, huge (1e6) coordinates and a comb of
18 triangles are all handled.

Defect found (blocking for the "Draw P / Draw Q" feature, fixed): the "Close polygon" validator only rejected
proper crossings. Accepted and then silently wrong:

- repeated consecutive vertex (rectangle with a doubled corner): triangulation returned 1 triangle, area lost 250 of 500;
- zero-width spike (a vertex going out and back along the same line): lost 250 of 500;
- pinched polygon touching itself at a vertex (bow-tie with shared point): 0 triangles, lost 500 of 500.

The animation then showed pieces not covering the figure while the label still said "Common area A = 500".
Fix applied: closed-segment intersection test (touching and collinear overlap count), rejection of back-tracking
adjacent edges, removal of consecutive duplicate points, and a final guard that the ear clipping returns exactly n-2
triangles. After the fix all of the above are refused with the existing "Invalid" message (re-run, worst area error
back to 1e-12 for every accepted case), and legitimate inputs (clockwise, collinear vertex, comb) are still accepted.

Other fixes applied (accessibility): canvas `role="img"` with an aria-label; aria-labels on the previous, next and
progress controls; `aria-live="polite"` on the step caption; under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing autoplays (pair
change, gallery click and custom polygon start paused).

Remaining, minor:

- Drawing a custom polygon is click/touch only; there is no keyboard path (presets and all other controls are keyboard
  operable, so the page is usable, but the drawing feature is not).
- Very thin custom polygons give an extremely wide common width (a 100 x 0.001 rectangle gives W of about 8840 units),
  so the pieces become hair-thin on screen. Correct but unreadable; a minimum aspect-ratio check would help.
- Piece counts grow quickly (a concave star to hexagon is 24 to 26 pieces per stage); the text already says the
  construction favours clarity over minimal piece count.
- The plain-text claim "any simple polygon admits an ear" is correct; the triangulation is O(n^3) with no vertex cap.
- English only, no French text.

## 2. set-theory-interactive-atlas.html

Mathematics, verified exhaustively. Universe of 4 elements, all 16^3 = 4096 triples (A, B, C), real `Set` operations
as the independent reference:

- all 15 laws in the Reference tab: truth table equal on both sides, and both sides equal the independent set expression
  on every triple; left/right predicates equal the elementwise result (no mismatch);
- all 8 two-set and 8 three-set operations of the Explore tab (incl. symmetric difference as parity) match union,
  intersection, difference, complement computed independently;
- the four extra shading prompts, including "at least two", match;
- 28 500 randomised quiz questions (all 15 generators x 1500, plus 12-element explore): every one has exactly one
  correct choice, no duplicate option text, and the correct option equals an independent recomputation parsed from the
  question context (operations, triple expressions, cardinality by inclusion-exclusion, subset, proper subset, disjoint,
  membership, 2^n, |A|x|B|);
- Venn geometry: the 8 label anchors lie in the right regions, all 8 regions of the three-circle layout are non-empty.

Lesson text checked: examples are right (|A union B| = 5 from 4, 3, 2; the empty-set, power-set and Cartesian examples;
notation of proper subset and complement). No mathematical error found.

Defects fixed:

1. Shade-the-diagram feedback never showed missed regions: the code chose a green colour for an expected-but-unshaded
   region but drew it with opacity 0, so after "Check shading" only wrongly shaded regions appeared (red). Missed
   regions now show in green at 0.38 opacity.
2. 21 SVG text elements in lesson diagrams used an invalid `font="26px Georgia"` attribute (ignored by browsers: text was
   drawn at the default size). Converted to `style="font:..."`; the Power set lesson was re-rendered and checked.
3. Clickable Venn SVGs had `role="img"`, which makes the focusable region buttons inside invisible to assistive
   technology. They now use `role="group"` when interactive (`img` otherwise).
4. Shading regions now expose `aria-pressed`.
5. Top tabs used `aria-selected` on plain buttons (invalid outside `role="tab"`): replaced by `aria-current`.

Remaining, minor:

- Shading correctness feedback (red/green/violet) relies on colour; the text line states only whether all match. Add a
  pattern or an outline for colour-blind use.
- Section tabs are plain buttons, not a full tab pattern (no arrow keys); acceptable but not ideal.
- 12 number toggles in Explore are 9 to 10 px font on very small screens (420 px media rule); still above 24 px target.
- English only. Reduced motion is respected (transitions off, smooth scroll off); there is no animation.

## 3. monte-carlo-lab.html

Mathematics, verified by running the page's own estimator and interval code (extracted without change) in node.
Reference values: pi exact; integrals by the page's Simpson rule against an independent high-resolution evaluation:
e^(-x^2) on [0,2] = 0.8820813908 (exact 0.8820813908), sin^2(pi x) on [0,1] = 0.5, narrow spike
e^(-650(x-0.72)^2) on [0,1] = 0.0695213598 (exact 0.0695213598); gambler's ruin L/(U+L) matches simulation for 5/3,
10/2, 2/10. The lake reference is the area of the polygon actually sampled (29.844 km^2 for the default shape; the smooth
curve it approximates gives 29.863), so the comparison is exact, not approximate.

Estimators are unbiased: means of 1500 to 4000 repeated runs match the references to Monte Carlo noise at every n.
Coverage of the stated "approximate 95% interval" (Wilson for pi, lake, walk; normal with sample variance for the
integral), 1500 to 4000 replications per cell:

| experiment | n=30 | n=100 | n=1000 | n=10000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pi | 92.5% | 94.9% | 94.8% | 94.5% |
| lake | 93.1% | 94.8% | 94.7% | 95.5% |
| e^(-x^2) | 94.1% | 94.8% | 95.0% | 93.4% |
| sin^2 | 94.2% | 95.1% | 94.6% | 95.3% |
| narrow spike | 85.5% | 92.2% | 95.1% | 95.3% |
| walk 5/3 | 94.1% | 93.9% | 95.3% | 94.2% |
| walk 10/2, 2/10 | 97.8% | 94.6%, 93.7% | 94.8% | 95.1%, 95.5% |

Conclusion: no mathematical defect. The only deviation is the expected one: the normal interval for the very skewed
narrow spike covers about 85% at n=30 and 92% at n=100. The page labels the interval "approximate" and the spike
exercise is designed to show this failure, but the caption could say "may be too narrow for rare, spiky functions".
The 1/sqrt(N) statement and the pseudocode are correct.

Fixes applied (accessibility only): `role="tablist"` on the experiment nav (the buttons already had `role="tab"`),
`role="img"` on the two canvases whose `aria-label` was otherwise ignored.

Remaining, minor:

- Lake drawing is pointer only. After redrawing there is no control to restore the default shoreline (reload needed).
  A keyboard alternative (preset shapes) and a "default shape" button are advisable before integration.
- Tabs have `role="tab"` but no arrow-key handling, no `aria-controls`.
- Run speed is "trials per animation frame", so it depends on the display refresh rate (15 per frame is 900 per second at
  60 Hz, 1800 at 120 Hz). `prefers-reduced-motion` only disables CSS transitions; the run is user-started, so acceptable.
- No `aria-live` on the estimate (deliberate? it updates every frame; a throttled announcement would be better).
- Self-intersection test in the lake drawing ignores touching edges (clamped points on the border can touch); the
  even-odd rule keeps the area well defined, and the reference uses the same polygon, so results stay consistent.
- Bilingual EN/FR in one file: when integrated, the language rule (curricula in one language each, bilingual goal on
  standby) has to be applied.

## 4. london-eye-interactive-exercise.html

Mathematics, verified in node and in the page (slider driven from 0 to 30 min in steps of 0.25, both viewports):

- h(t) = 75 - 60 cos(pi t / 15): a = -60, b = pi/15, d = 75; h(0) = 15, h(15) = 135, h(30) = 15.
- h(5) = 45 exactly.
- First time at 120 m: t1 = (15/pi) arccos(-3/4) = 11.5492 (shown 11.55); second crossing 30 - t1 = 18.4508.
- Time at or above 120 m: 6.9016 (sampled at 1e-5 min: 6.90161), shown 6.90.
- h'(t) = 4 pi sin(pi t / 15); numerical derivative maximum 12.56637 at t = 7.4999 (4 pi = 12.56637 at 7.5 min); the
  signed-rate remark (+12.57 at 7.5, -12.57 at 22.5) is right.
- Displayed height and rate match the formulas within display rounding (max 0.005); seat position on the wheel and dot
  on the graph both give the same height to 1e-13; the above/at/below 120 m notice is right at every sample.
- Dragging maps the pointer to t in [0, 30) correctly; the wheel turns clockwise, as the real one does.

Published dimensions: 135 m overall height, 120 m diameter, 32 capsules and "approximately 30 minutes" are all labelled
"Published"/"Reported data", the period carries "approximately" or the tilde sign, and a separate caveat states that the
15 m minimum, 75 m midline, exact 30 minutes and lowest-point start are exercise assumptions, not measurements of the
real capsules (capsules are outside the rim, upright). Sources are linked, no external resource loaded. Satisfactory.

No defect. Remaining, trivial:

- In the wheel diagram the ground line is drawn at y = 353 while the common scale of the graph puts 0 m at y = 349 (about
  1.8 m of drawing offset); the seat, centre and 120 m guide are consistent with the graph scale and the alignment guide.
- Dragging the seat is pointer only; the time slider and the graph click remain keyboard operable (slider), the graph
  scrub is not. Seven small links/controls are under 24 px (inline source links).
- English only.
